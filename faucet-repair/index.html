When a faucet or valve problem is an emergency

Emergency faucet repair in Dixon, CA applies when water will not stop. That means a faucet that runs after it is turned off, a broken or leaking shutoff valve under the sink, or a split supply line. Our line is answered 24/7, and a licensed plumber can come the same day when availability allows.

Look under the sink for the small oval valves on the cold and hot lines and turn them clockwise. If they will not turn or keep leaking, shut off the main water valve to the house and call.

Which fixture is it and what usually fails

Fixtures wear out in recognizable patterns. This is how a plumber sorts a dripping faucet from a failed valve.

Drip from the spout

A worn cartridge, washer or seat. The faucet type decides the fix.

Leak at the handle or base

A worn seal, O-ring or loose packing nut.

Shower or tub valve drips

A failing cartridge inside the wall valve, which is a more involved repair.

Water under the sink

A loose compression nut, a cracked supply line or a leaking drain connection.

Shutoff valve will not turn or drips

An old angle stop that has corroded or seized.

A drip that keeps coming back after a repair is a good reason to review the supply valve, and the leak detection page covers hidden supply leaks.

Dixon hard water, aerators and CalGREEN flow limits

Source type: City of Dixon water quality report and permit handouts.

The City of Dixon's 2024 water quality report lists total hardness averaging 163 parts per million. Hard water leaves mineral scale in aerators and cartridges, which causes sputtering spouts, weak flow and stiff handles. A vinegar soak often clears an aerator, and a new cartridge restores a stiff handle.

The City of Dixon's handouts cite CalGREEN section 4.303, which sets flow limits on new and replaced fixtures: 1.2 gallons per minute for bathroom faucets, 1.8 for kitchen faucets and 1.8 for showerheads. A replacement faucet in Dixon should be labeled to those limits.

Repairing leaks in valves and fixtures is listed as exempt from permits in the same handouts, as long as pipes and fixtures are not replaced or rearranged.

Fixture work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). CalGREEN, the California Green Building Standards Code, adds the flow limits in section 4.303 that the City of Dixon's handout lists.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A few quick steps keep a faucet or valve problem from becoming a flood.

  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink, or the main valve if they fail.
  • Place a bucket or towels under any active drip.
  • Do not use pliers on a chrome finish, since they scratch and slip.
  • Do not force a stuck valve, because a broken stem makes the leak worse.
  • Photograph any water damage for the insurer.

Shower valves, tub spouts and supply lines

A shower valve is mounted inside the wall, so replacing a cartridge may mean opening an access panel or working through the trim. If the valve keeps dripping into the wall, or you see staining on the other side of the wall, the repair is larger than a drip.

Braided supply lines under sinks usually have a service life. A line that is bulging, rusty at the nut or older than the faucet should be replaced when the fixture is serviced, because a failure under the sink is a flood in the cabinet.
