Plumbing emergency in Vacaville: what to do right now

If water is spreading or a drain has backed up in Vacaville, call our 24/7 emergency line first. A licensed plumber can come the same day when availability allows. While you wait, these steps keep the damage small.

  1. Close the main water valve

    Turn it clockwise at the house or near the meter to stop a burst line.

  2. Stop using sinks, tubs and toilets

    A backed-up drain sends more water to the lowest opening.

  3. Turn off the water heater

    Switch off the power or set a gas unit to pilot if the tank is leaking.

  4. Move valuables and photograph the damage

    Photos help with an insurance claim later.

  5. Call and describe what you see

    Mention where the water is, what you have shut off and any smell of gas.

If you smell natural gas, leave the home and call 9-1-1 before anything else.

Vacaville water: three sources and a hard mineral load

Source type: City of Vacaville 2024 water quality report.

The City of Vacaville's 2024 report shows that 58 percent of its water comes from Lake Berryessa through the Putah South Canal, 9 percent from the North Bay Aqueduct and 33 percent from seven deep wells. Hardness averages between 163 and 177 parts per million depending on the source.

That is well above the level where scale builds on fixtures and heating elements. Water heaters, shower valves and aerators in Vacaville homes collect mineral deposits over the years. A tank that is flushed and a heater that is replaced on time cope better, as the water heater replacement page explains.

For questions about your water service or a leak on the city side of the meter, the City of Vacaville utilities line is (707) 469-6400.

On the U.S. Geological Survey scale, 121 to 180 parts per million is hard. Vacaville's 163 to 177 average is hard water, which is why scale shows up in heaters and fixtures.

Problems we see in an older, fast-growing city

Vacaville grew from 10,898 residents in 1960 to 71,479 in 1990, and the 2020 census counted 102,386. Homes built in those decades are now between about 35 and 65 years old, which is when original plumbing begins to show its age.

Aging water heaters

A tank past its warranty fails suddenly, often with a leak at the base.

Clogged main lines

Older sewer laterals collect roots and scale, and slow drains are the early sign.

Pinhole leaks in supply pipe

Repeated leaks point to pipe wearing thin from the inside.

Heat-related stress

Summer highs above 100 degrees push water heaters and irrigation lines hard.

Slow drains after storms

Wet winters, about 25 inches of rain a year, test yard drains and cleanouts.

A pattern of leaks is the reason people consider repiping, and a slow main drain is covered under sewer line cleaning.
