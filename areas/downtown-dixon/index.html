Downtown Dixon: where the town started

Downtown Dixon grew from the railroad. The LocalWiki page on Dixon says the city was first established by the tracks near West A Street and North Lincoln Street. The Central Pacific railroad arrived in 1868, and the town moved to meet it.

Dixon was incorporated in 1878. On November 19, 1883, a fire destroyed much of the town, and a later ordinance called for brick or tin buildings. The Dixon Carnegie Library at 135 East B Street, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2011, is one of the older buildings that remains.

What older blocks mean for plumbing

Older blocks often mix uses. Lines and drains can be shared between neighboring units, and past remodels may have changed the layout. A plumber should trace the line before cutting.

A business water emergency: the first five minutes

Water inside a shop or office can ruin stock and floors in minutes. Do these in order.

  1. Close the main valve

    Ask a building manager where it is if the building is shared.

  2. Stop the flow at the fixture

    Turn off the supply under a sink or behind a toilet.

  3. Cut power near wet areas only if it is safe

    Do not touch outlets or panels while standing in water.

  4. Lift stock and records off the floor

    Move them to shelves or tables.

  5. Photograph the damage and call

    Photos help with insurance, and the dispatcher will ask where the water is.

If you smell natural gas, leave the building and call 9-1-1 first.

When the problem is on the street side of the meter

Source type: City of Dixon utilities contact page.

The city, not a plumber, handles the water main and the service line up to the meter. The City of Dixon water operations number is (707) 678-7050.

For after-hours city-side emergencies, the city lists (707) 676-3005. City Hall is at 600 East A Street, and the main number is (707) 678-7000.

Stopping a leak or clearing a stoppage is exempt from permits, according to the city's handout. Replacing a concealed pipe with new pipe is not.

What to have ready when you call

A short list helps the dispatcher send the right plumber.

  • The street address and suite or unit number.
  • Where the water or the backup is, and what you have shut off.
  • The name of the landlord or property manager if the building is shared.
  • Whether the building is open and who can let a plumber in.
