Davis water changed with a new source, and it still matters

Source type: City of Davis 2024 water quality report.

The City of Davis now draws about 80 percent of its water from the Sacramento River through the Woodland-Davis Clean Water Agency, and about 20 percent from five deep wells. Its weighted average hardness was 66 parts per million in 2024. In 2015, before surface water arrived, it was 306.

That is a large drop. A home with a softener tuned to the old water may now over-treat it, which is why the City of Davis suggests that softener users bypass or adjust their units. Scale left from the old water can remain in heaters and fixtures for years.

Water in Davis is softer than in Dixon, where hardness averages 163 parts per million, so scale-related calls differ between the two towns. Our water softener page explains how to think about it.

On the U.S. Geological Survey scale, 61 to 120 parts per million is moderately hard. Davis's 2024 average of 66 sits near the bottom of that band, a big change from 306, which was very hard.

Davis homes, hard and soft water side by side

Because the water supply changed in the middle of many homes' lives, plumbing here has two histories. This comparison shows how they play out.

Homes with legacy scale

  • Aerators and showerheads clog with old deposits.
  • Water heater tanks may hold sediment from the earlier water.
  • Softeners may be set for hardness that is no longer there.
  • Older faucets may stick or drip from mineral wear.

Homes with newer plumbing

  • Less scale on fixtures and heating elements.
  • Heaters may last closer to their rated life.
  • Softeners are often optional.
  • Problems are more likely from age or pressure than from minerals.

Burst pipe or active leak in Davis: before we arrive

These steps keep a Davis leak small until the plumber is at the door. Start them as soon as you have called the 24/7 line.

  1. Close the main water valve

    Know the path to it and clear it before the plumber arrives.

  2. Cut power to wet areas

    Switch off breakers for rooms with standing water, and stay out of water near outlets.

  3. Clear the area

    Move people, pets, rugs and valuables away from the wet floor.

  4. Photograph the damage

    Take pictures of wet areas and damaged items for the insurer.

  5. Note what you tried

    Write down when it started so the plumber can start with the right question.
