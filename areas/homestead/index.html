Three things to find on move-in day

A new owner often has never touched the plumbing. Spend ten minutes finding these before a problem forces the issue.

  1. The main water shutoff

    Look near the water meter, at the front of the house or in the garage, and turn it once to confirm it moves.

  2. The water heater label

    It lists the install date, the fuel type and the tank size, which a plumber will ask for.

  3. The shutoff under each sink and behind each toilet

    A small valve there stops a single fixture without shutting off the house.

Write the three locations on a note inside a kitchen cabinet so anyone in the home can find them.

What Dixon water does to new fixtures

Source type: City of Dixon 2024 water quality report.

Hard water starts working on a new house on the first day. Dixon's 2024 average is 163 parts per million, and a new faucet or showerhead is not protected from it.

Expect a white film on glass shower doors and chrome. Wiping with vinegar removes light scale, and a softener prevents it from forming in the first place.

The U.S. Department of Energy advises flushing a water heater in hard water. Put that on the calendar early, since new homes have no history of the tank being flushed.

The U.S. Geological Survey calls 121 to 180 parts per million hard, and Dixon's 163 average falls inside it.

Open space nearby and why it matters for irrigation

KB Home describes about 30 acres of open space with parks and trails at Copperleaf at Homestead. Richmond American notes proximity to the Vacaville-Dixon Greenbelt.

A new yard often means a new irrigation line and hose bibs. A leak at a hose bib or a valve box shows as a soggy patch or a water bill that climbs without an explanation. Call us for leak detection if the bill jumps and you cannot see why.
