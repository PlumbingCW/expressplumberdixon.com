Problems that follow a house past thirty

A home from the early 1990s has seen most of its original parts wear out once. These are the calls that come up for houses that age.

A water heater on its second or third life

The tank in your garage may not be the original. Check the label for its year before it fails.

A dripping tub or shower valve

Seals wear over decades. A drip that keeps returning after the faucet is shut off points to the valve.

A main drain that slows after rain

Roots can enter older lines. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe.

Seized shutoff valves

Valves that have not been turned in decades may leak when you first move them. Turn them gently once a year.

Hose bibs that drip inside the wall

A hose bib that leaks only when a hose is attached can wet the wall behind it.

Slow drains in a single fixture are a drain cleaning call, and a drain that is slow everywhere points to the main line.

Garage water heaters and the City of Dixon's rules

Source type: City of Dixon water heater permit handout.

Many Dixon homes keep the water heater in the garage. The city handout lists rules for that location, and a plumber should follow them on any replacement.

A burner in a garage must sit at least 18 inches above the floor, which is section 507.13 of the California Plumbing Code. The handout also calls for 30 inches of working space in front of the unit.

Seismic strapping uses two straps, one in the upper third and one in the lower third of the tank. The temperature and pressure relief line must run to the outside, ending 6 to 24 inches above the ground.

These section numbers come from the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
