Business plumbing emergency: what to do first

Emergency commercial plumbing in Dixon, CA starts with a call to our 24/7 line. A licensed plumber can come the same day when availability allows, including after hours and on weekends. These steps limit damage while you wait.

  1. Shut off the main water valve

    Know where the building's main is before an emergency happens.

  2. Keep customers and staff out of wet areas

    Wet floors and electrical equipment are a safety risk.

  3. Stop using drains and restrooms

    A backed-up line sends water to the lowest fixture, so stop adding to it.

  4. Call the 24/7 line and describe the problem

    Mention the building type, the symptom and whether the main valve is closed.

  5. Photograph damage for the insurer

    Take photos before cleanup and keep receipts.

Common commercial plumbing problems

Commercial buildings run more fixtures and use them harder than homes. These problems account for most business calls.

Backed-up floor drains

A main line clog, often from grease, wipes or roots, sends water to the lowest drain.

Grease trap overflow

A full trap in a food business sends fats into the line and out of the floor sink.

Burst or leaking supply line

A failed line in a ceiling or wall can flood several rooms.

No hot water

A failed or undersized water heater affects kitchens, restrooms and cleaning.

Failed backflow assembly

A test failure can hold up water service or a business license.

Most line clogs are handled with the methods on our hydro jetting page, which clears grease and scale from larger lines.

Dixon rules that matter to building owners

Source type: City of Dixon Building Division permit handouts and water operations contacts.

The City of Dixon's permit handouts require cleanouts at a sewer line's property line, at its end and at set intervals, and call for a backwater valve where fixtures sit below the next upstream manhole cover. A business that is ever flooded by a sewer backup should ask whether its fixtures sit below that level.

Backflow assemblies protect the drinking supply from irrigation, boilers and other equipment. Rules on testing come from the water supplier, so ask the City of Dixon water operations office at (707) 678-7050 what applies to your building.

For water emergencies on the city's side of the meter, the City of Dixon lists an after-hours emergency line at (707) 676-3005. Plumbing on your side of the meter is your responsibility, and that is when you call us.

Commercial work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The City of Dixon's handouts cite section 719.1 for cleanouts and section 710.1 for backwater valves.

Grease traps and routine maintenance

Food businesses depend on grease traps and interceptors to keep fats out of the sewer. A trap that is cleaned on a schedule rarely causes trouble, and one that is ignored overflows at the worst moment, usually during a rush.

Ask your plumber to inspect the trap, the floor sinks and the main line on a routine visit. A camera inspection of the main line, described on our sewer inspection page, shows scale and root intrusion before a backup closes the doors.
