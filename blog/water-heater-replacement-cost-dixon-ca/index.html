Water Heater Replacement Cost in Dixon, CA

The final price depends on the tank, the fuel, the permit and what the old setup needs. This guide shows what to compare when you collect quotes.

By the Dixon Express Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026. Updated October 13, 2026.

The short answer

There is no single price for a water heater replacement in Dixon, because the job depends on the heater you choose and the home you have. A like-for-like tank swap in an easy location is the simplest case. A change of fuel, a tankless unit or a move to a new location adds work.

We do not publish a flat number, since any figure on a web page ignores your layout. What you can do is learn which factors move the price, and use them to compare quotes. Call (916) 312-0578 for a written price based on your home.

Seven factors that set the price

Plumbers build a quote from the same few pieces. Knowing them lets you see why two quotes differ.

Tank size and typeLarger tanks and higher efficiency models cost more than basic ones.
Fuel and ventingGas units need venting and a gas connection, electric ones need the right circuit.
LocationA garage is easier than an attic, a closet or a crawlspace.
Code upgradesExpansion tanks, seismic straps and pans may be needed to meet code.
Permit and inspectionDixon requires permits for many water heater changeouts.
Haul-awayRemoving and disposing of the old tank can be a separate line.
TimingAn emergency or after-hours visit may be priced differently from a scheduled one.

What the City of Dixon requires

Source type: City of Dixon instant permit handout for water heater changeouts.

The City of Dixon's instant permit handout covers direct replacements of the same kind, function and capacity. A gas tank swapped for a tankless or heat pump unit does not qualify, and neither does work that needs new gas piping or new electrical.

The handout lists code items that plumbers must handle, including two seismic straps on the tank and a hard-piped temperature and pressure relief line to the outside. They also include a secondary pan where a leak could cause damage and a new gas flex connector with an accessible shutoff. These steps are part of the job and part of the price.

The code items in the City of Dixon handout come from the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The strap rule is section 507.2 and the relief line rule is section 504.6.

What to do if your heater fails before you have a quote

A failing heater does not wait for a quote. These steps keep the situation safe while you compare.

  • Turn off the power at the breaker or set a gas unit to pilot.
  • Close the cold water valve on top of the tank if it is leaking.
  • Do not touch wet wiring or stand in water near the unit.
  • Use a bucket or wet vacuum to keep water from spreading.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call 9-1-1 before anything else.

Questions to ask every plumber

Use the same questions with each quote. If one plumber leaves an item out, ask why.

  • Is the permit and inspection included.
  • Are the straps, relief line and pan included.
  • What is the age and warranty of the new heater.
  • Is haul-away of the old unit included.
  • Who is responsible if the inspector requires a change.

California protections when you hire

The Contractors State License Board says a contractor license is required for work totaling $1,000 or more in labor and materials. You can check a license at cslb.ca.gov or by calling 800-321-2752. A down payment is capped at 10 percent of the price or $1,000, whichever is less. You have three days to cancel a home improvement contract, or five if you are 65 or older.

Keep every quote in writing. A written scope lets you compare plumbers on the same job.

Comparing two quotes fairly

Suppose two plumbers quote the same gas tank. One includes the permit, the straps, the relief line, a new pan and haul-away. The other lists only the heater and a single labor line. The second quote looks cheaper, but it leaves out work the City of Dixon expects.

Put both quotes side by side and mark which items each one covers. Ask the second plumber to add the missing items in writing. The honest comparison is the total for the same finished job, not the first number on the page.

When a repair beats a replacement

Not every failing heater needs to be replaced. The age of the tank and the nature of the fault decide.

Repair may be enough when

  • The tank is under about ten years old.
  • The fault is a thermostat, element or valve.
  • There is no leak from the tank itself.
  • The warranty still applies.

Replacement is usually better when

  • The tank is leaking from the body.
  • The unit is near the end of the 10 to 15 year range.
  • Repairs have been frequent.
  • Sediment has built up badly from hard water.

Planning ahead

If your heater is more than ten years old, start planning. Note the make, model and age from the label. Look for rust at the base, moisture on the floor or a rumbling noise when it heats. Those are signs the tank is aging.

Collect quotes on a calm day, and decide before the heater fails. You will have more choices and less pressure.

Replace now or wait for it to fail

The Department of Energy says storage tank heaters generally last 10 to 15 years. A tank at the end of that range can fail with a flood, which turns a planned expense into an emergency with water damage.
