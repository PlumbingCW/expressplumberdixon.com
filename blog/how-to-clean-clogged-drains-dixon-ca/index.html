How to Clean a Clogged Drain at Home in Dixon, CA

Most single-fixture clogs can be cleared with a plunger or a snake. Here are the steps and the signs that it is time to call.

By the Dixon Express Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026. Updated October 13, 2026.

24/7 emergency line.(916) 312-0578Call now.

The short answer

A clog in one fixture can usually be cleared with a plunger or a small drain snake. A clog that affects several fixtures, or comes back, is in the main line and needs a plumber.

Start with the least aggressive method, protect the fixture and stop if you meet resistance you cannot move. Our drain cleaning page covers what we do when home methods are not enough.

Clearing a sink or tub step by step

Gather a cup plunger, a bucket, rubber gloves and a small hand snake. Follow these steps in order.

  1. Remove the stopper or strainer

    Pull out hair and debris by hand first.

  2. Seal the overflow

    Cover the overflow hole with a wet rag so the plunger can build pressure.

  3. Plunge with firm strokes

    Use ten or fifteen pushes, keeping the cup submerged.

  4. Use a drain snake if it persists

    Feed it in, turn the handle and pull out what it catches.

  5. Flush with hot water

    Run the tap for a minute and check the flow.

Local drain context in Dixon

Source type: City of Dixon Building Division permit handouts.

The City of Dixon's permit handouts list clearing a stoppage as work that does not need a permit. A plumber can clear a main line without a permit visit, which keeps same-day service simple.

The handouts also require cleanouts at set points on a sewer line, including the property line and the end of the line. If you can find your cleanout, a plumber can reach the main line without removing a fixture. Knowing where it is saves time during an emergency.

Cleanouts are a code item. The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for them at set points on a sewer line, and the City of Dixon's handout cites section 719.1.

Which tool for which clog

Match the tool to the symptom.

Slow sink with standing water

A cup plunger, then a hand snake.

Slow shower or tub

A hair-catching tool, then a snake through the overflow.

Clogged toilet

A flange plunger, not a cup plunger.

Kitchen sink with a disposal

Clear the disposal first and check the trap under the sink.

Multiple fixtures backing up

Stop. This is a main line problem.

When home methods fit and when they do not

Know where to stop so you do not damage the plumbing.

Home methods fit when

  • Only one fixture is slow.
  • Water drains, just slowly.
  • The clog is near the opening.
  • You can remove and clean the trap.

Call a plumber when

  • Water backs up in another fixture.
  • The clog returns after a few days.
  • You smell sewage.
  • A snake meets a hard stop.

Using a hand snake

Feed the cable into the drain until you meet resistance. Tighten the thumbscrew, turn the handle clockwise and push gently. When the cable moves freely, pull it back slowly, since it often brings hair and debris with it.

Wipe the cable as it comes out, and run hot water to confirm the flow. If the cable stops against something hard and will not pass, stop. Forcing it can scratch pipes or push the clog deeper.

Clearing a toilet clog

Use a flange plunger and make sure the bowl has enough water to cover the cup. Push slowly at first to seat the seal, then pump firmly for about twenty seconds. Repeat a few times before judging the result.

If the water stays high, a toilet auger reaches the bend in the trap. Do not flush again until the water drops. A toilet that clogs often, or backs up with another fixture, points to a line problem, and our toilet repair page explains the checks.

After the drain clears

Run hot water for a minute and watch for any slow spots. Clean the stopper and strainer, wipe the area and put the tools away. Note the date, because a clog that returns within a week or two has a cause upstream.

If you needed the snake more than once this season, a deeper cleaning is worth pricing. Our hydro jetting page covers cleaning the whole line with high pressure water.

Plunger or snake: which first

Start with the less invasive tool and move up. Each one works best in a certain situation.

Start with a plunger when

  • Water is standing in the sink or tub.
  • The clog is close to the opening.
  • The fixture drains slowly rather than not at all.
  • You have not used chemicals in the drain.

Move to a snake when

  • The plunger moves the water but it returns.
  • Hair is likely the cause.
  • The drain gurgles after plunging.
  • The clog seems farther down the pipe.

Know when to stop

Plungers and hand snakes are safe in the right hands, but they have limits. If the water does not move after two honest attempts, stop. A third and fourth attempt rarely work and can damage a fixture or push the clog farther.

Tell the plumber what you tried and what you saw. That history shortens the diagnosis and helps them choose the right tool.

Keep a bucket and old towels nearby while you work. Water in the trap spills when you remove it, and a little preparation keeps the cleanup short. Wear gloves, since drain buildup is unpleasant and can carry bacteria.

Prevention for Dixon homes

Keep grease out of the kitchen sink, use hair catchers in showers and never flush wipes, even labeled flushable ones. Run hot water after washing dishes to move grease along. Hard water leaves scum in the pipe, so a periodic cleaning of traps helps.

If clogs keep returning, a camera inspection shows what is going on inside the line. See our sewer camera inspection page for details. We serve Dixon, Vacaville and Davis.
