Sewer Line Repair Cost in Dixon, CA

Sewer repair prices vary because every line is different. This guide covers what drives the number and what Dixon requires.

By the Dixon Express Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026. Updated October 13, 2026.

24/7 emergency line.(916) 312-0578Call now.

The short answer

Sewer line repair prices depend on the length of pipe, the depth, the method and what sits on top of the line. A short spot repair in an open yard is the simple end. A long run under a driveway or a tree is the expensive end.

A camera inspection comes first, so the plumber can show you the damage and quote it. Call (916) 312-0578 to book. Our sewer line repair page covers the methods we offer.

What Dixon requires for sewer work

Source type: City of Dixon sewer lateral permit handout.

The City of Dixon's sewer lateral handout covers liner and trenchless methods, and requires camera testing for trenchless work and liners. It calls for cleanouts at the drain and sewer junction, at the property line, at the end of the line, at changes of direction beyond 135 degrees and every 100 feet.

A backwater valve is required where fixtures sit below the next upstream manhole cover. Testing includes a water test with a 10 foot head for 15 minutes, or an air test at 5 psi for 15 minutes on non-plastic pipe. Each of these steps shows up in the scope and the price.

Sewer repair follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The City of Dixon's handouts cite section 719.1 for cleanouts, 710.1 for backwater valves and 708.1 for interior slope.

Questions to ask before approving the work

Ask each plumber for answers in writing.

  • What did the camera show and can I see the video.
  • Which method is quoted and why.
  • Is the permit and inspection included.
  • What restoration is included.
  • What warranty applies.

The Contractors State License Board lists a license requirement for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials. Check a license at cslb.ca.gov.

How the camera inspection works

A plumber feeds a small waterproof camera into the line through a cleanout. The camera sends live video to a screen, showing cracks, offset joints, roots, sags and scale. A locator can mark the surface above any problem.

Ask to see the video and ask the plumber to point out what is wrong and where. A clear recording is the best basis for any quote, and you can show it to a second plumber for comparison.

From slow drain to full backup

Sewer problems rarely appear overnight. A slow drain, a gurgle or a faint odor can show up weeks before the line backs up. Early inspection often costs less than waiting for an emergency.

A full backup floods the lowest fixture in the house and can damage floors. If it happens, stop using water and call the 24/7 line. Our sewer line cleaning page explains how we clear a blocked line.

Tree roots in sewer lines

Roots look for moisture and enter a line through small cracks and loose joints. Once inside, they grow and catch debris. Cutting the roots clears the line for a while, but they return unless the opening is repaired.

If the camera shows roots at a joint, a spot repair or lining can seal the entry point. A plumber can explain which option fits the pipe's condition.

Planning for the disruption

A sewer repair affects your yard, driveway or garage floor, depending on where the pipe runs. Ask the plumber where the dig or pits will be, how long they will stay open and who restores the surface.

Plan for a day or more without full use of the plumbing. Know where the nearest public restroom is, and ask whether the plumber can restore service at the end of each day. Clear the work area of vehicles and furniture before the crew arrives.

Getting more than one opinion

A sewer repair is large enough to justify a second quote. Share the camera video with the second plumber so both quote the same problem.

Compare the method, the scope, the permit and the restoration, not just the total. The cheapest quote may skip a step that you pay for later.

Ask whether the plumber carries insurance and whether the work is warranted in writing. A repair under a driveway or foundation is not something you want to dispute later. Keep the contract, the permit and the final inspection report together in one folder.

Insurance and who is responsible

The homeowner is usually responsible for the sewer lateral from the house to the connection with the city main. Check the property line cleanout and your policy. Some insurers offer line coverage as an add-on, and many exclude root damage or wear.

If you are unsure where your responsibility ends, ask the City of Dixon Building Division before paying for repairs. We serve Dixon, Vacaville and Davis. For a clog without damage, see sewer line cleaning.
