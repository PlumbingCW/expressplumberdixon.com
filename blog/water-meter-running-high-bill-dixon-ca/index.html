Water Meter Running With Everything Off in Dixon, CA

A meter that moves with everything off is the clearest sign of a leak. This guide shows a simple test and the next steps.

By the Dixon Express Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026. Updated October 13, 2026.

24/7 emergency line.(916) 312-0578Call now.

The short answer

If the water meter keeps moving after you shut off every fixture, water is leaking somewhere. It can be a running toilet, an irrigation valve or a buried line. The meter test tells you whether to look further.

The next step is finding which side of the main valve the leak is on. Our leak detection page explains how we find the exact spot without digging blindly.

The five minute meter test

Do this when no one is using water.

  1. Turn off every fixture and appliance

    Include the dishwasher, washing machine and ice maker.

  2. Find your meter

    Lift the lid of the meter box, usually near the street.

  3. Read the low flow indicator

    A small dial or triangle that spins with even tiny flow.

  4. Wait fifteen minutes and read again

    A moving indicator or changed reading means a leak.

  5. Close the main valve and look again

    If it stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, it is before the valve.

The City of Dixon's role and your role

Source type: City of Dixon utility contacts and 2024 water quality report.

The City of Dixon lists utility billing at (707) 678-7008 and water operations at (707) 678-7050. After hours, the city lists an emergency line at (707) 676-3005. Ask billing for your recent usage history so you can see when the jump began.

The city's 2024 report lists system pressure between 52 and 62 pounds per square inch. That steady pressure forces water through a leak around the clock, which is why even a small leak can show up on a bill. The leak on your side of the meter is your responsibility, and the city handles its side.

Common sources of a hidden leak

Start with the easy ones. They are the most common.

A running toilet

Check with a dye test in the tank.

A dripping faucet or shower

Even a slow drip adds up over a month.

An irrigation valve stuck open

Look for a wet patch or a valve box full of water.

A water heater relief line

A line that drips means pressure or temperature is too high.

A buried line to the house

Wet soil between the meter and the house.

Fixture leaks are covered on our toilet repair page, and buried lines on water line repair.

Compare bills month to month

Pull out several recent bills and compare usage, not just the dollar amount. A sudden jump with no change in the household points to a leak. A gradual rise over several months can point to a slow leak or a worn toilet.

Note the season too. Irrigation raises summer usage in many homes. If your bill climbs in the cooler months and the lawn is dormant, that is a stronger sign something is leaking.

Check irrigation and outdoor taps

Walk the yard with the sprinklers on and off. Look for soggy spots, geysers or valve boxes full of water. A stuck valve can run all night. Turn the controller to off and rerun the meter test.

Check outdoor hose bibs, too. A hose left connected with a leaking nozzle, or a bib that drips inside the wall, can waste water for weeks.

Appliances that leak quietly

Ice makers, refrigerator water lines, dishwasher hoses and washing machine hoses can leak behind the appliance. Pull the unit out and look for damp floors or stains. Check the shutoff valves for drips too.

If you find a leak behind a fixture, shut off the supply and call. Our faucet and valve repair page and disposal repair page cover the sink side.

When to call a plumber

If the meter test shows a leak and you cannot find it, call. A plumber has pressure gauges and listening gear that find hidden leaks without opening walls. That saves time and avoids damage.

If you find the leak and it is a simple fixture, you may repair it yourself. If it is behind a wall, under a floor or in the yard, a plumber should handle it. Our leak detection page explains the process, and the 24/7 line can book it today.

Keep a log

Write down the meter reading morning and evening for a few days. A steady rise with no use tells you a leak is running. Add notes about irrigation, guests and appliances.

That log helps the plumber narrow the search and gives the utility something concrete if you ask about a bill adjustment.

If the log shows the meter moving even at night, the leak is constant. If it moves only at certain times, look for something on a timer, such as irrigation or a softener cycle.

Insurance and bill adjustments

Some utilities offer a one-time adjustment for a leak that has been repaired, but policies vary. Ask City of Dixon utility billing what documentation they accept. Keep the repair invoice, photos and the plumber's note.

Your homeowner policy may cover resulting damage but often excludes the water itself. Call your agent before the work starts. We serve Dixon, Vacaville and Davis.
