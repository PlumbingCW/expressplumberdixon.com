How a tankless water heater works in a Dixon home

A tankless water heater in Dixon, CA heats water only when a tap is open. Cold water enters the unit, passes through a heat exchanger and leaves hot, so there is no tank of stored water losing heat in the background.

The U.S. Department of Energy reports that tankless units last about 20 years, compared with 10 to 15 for storage tanks, and that they are 8 to 34 percent more efficient depending on how much hot water a home uses. Output is typically 2 to 5 gallons per minute, so the unit has to be sized for how many fixtures run together.

Symptoms that mean a tankless unit needs service

Tankless problems feel different from tank problems. Most trace back to scale, flow or venting rather than a failed tank.

Cold water bursts between hot water

A partly clogged heat exchanger or a flow sensor issue causes unstable temperature.

Error code on the display

Codes point at ignition, flow, venting or temperature faults, and the manual lists them.

Water takes longer to heat or never gets hot

Scale narrows the heat exchanger and cuts heat transfer.

Unit shuts off with small flows

Tankless units need a minimum flow of about 0.5 gallons per minute to fire.

Noise or odd smells during a run

Combustion or venting problems need a technician, and a gas smell means call your gas utility.

Flush and descale: why Dixon water makes it a routine job

Source type: City of Dixon water quality report and U.S. Department of Energy guidance.

The City of Dixon's 2024 water quality report lists total hardness averaging 163 parts per million, or about 9.5 grains per gallon. That is hard water, and heat makes it deposit scale on the inside of a tankless heat exchanger.

The U.S. Department of Energy advises flushing a tankless unit with a descaling solution in hard water areas. A flush circulates the solution through the heat exchanger to dissolve scale, and then clear water is run through to rinse it. Many homes in hard water areas need this on a regular schedule.

Ask the plumber to check the unit's manual for the manufacturer's flush interval, and ask whether isolation valves are installed so the flush can be done without cutting pipe. A softener upstream cuts scale at the source, which our water softener installation page covers.

Tankless repair: what usually gets fixed

The most common tankless repairs are flushing scale, cleaning the inlet filter, replacing a flow sensor or igniter and correcting a vent problem. A unit with an error code is easier to diagnose than one that just seems off, so write the code down before you call.

If a unit was installed by another company, ask whether it has isolation valves and what the manufacturer's service interval is. A plumber can add valves during a visit, which makes every future flush quicker.
