What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer camera inspection in Dixon, CA sends a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable through the cleanout and along the main line. The screen shows the pipe wall, the joints and anything that does not belong, from roots to a sagging low spot to a crack.

Plumbers call the result a sewer scope. It turns a vague symptom, like a drain that clogs twice a year, into a clear statement of what the pipe looks like and what, if anything, needs to be done.

When a camera look is worth scheduling

Most inspections are planned rather than urgent. These are the situations where a camera run saves the most money.

Before buying a house

An older home can hide a failing line that a standard inspection never checks.

After repeat backups

A camera shows whether roots, a sag or a break keeps catching waste.

Before a remodel or new slab

You learn the condition of the line before you cover or build over it.

After a repair or lining

The City of Dixon expects lined and trenchless repairs to be camera tested.

How an inspection works, step by step

The visit is short, and you can usually watch the screen. Most of the work is deciding where to enter and what to record.

  1. Locate the cleanout

    The plumber finds the cleanout or removes a toilet if there is no direct access.

  2. Run the camera

    The camera travels toward the sewer lateral while the screen shows the pipe.

  3. Note defects

    Roots, cracks, sags, offset joints and scale are marked and described.

  4. Review and recommend

    You see the findings and hear whether the pipe needs cleaning, lining, repair or nothing.

What the camera can find and what it usually means

Each finding points toward a different next step. This table is a plain-language reading guide.

What you seeWhat it usually meansNext step
Root hairs at a jointA joint has opened enough for roots to enterCut the roots, then consider lining or a spot repair.
Standing water in a sectionA sag or belly where waste settlesMonitor, jet, or repair the low section.
Cracked or broken pipeA defect that cleaning cannot fixSpot repair, lining or replacement.
Flaking pipe wallCorrosion inside older cast ironCleaning now, repair planning for later.
Clean pipe, normal flowNo problem in the lineKeep the video for your records.

Camera testing and the City of Dixon

Source type: City of Dixon Building Division sewer lateral and permit handouts.

The City of Dixon's sewer lateral handout calls for camera testing on trenchless and lined sewer repairs. That makes a pre-repair camera look a natural first step, because you learn the condition of the pipe before choosing a method.

The same handout lists where cleanouts belong on a service line and notes that a backwater valve is required when fixtures sit below the next upstream manhole cover. A camera run through an existing cleanout also shows whether the line has the cleanouts the city expects.

A camera is also how a plumber confirms code items. The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets cleanouts under section 719.1 and a backwater valve under section 710.1, as the City of Dixon's handout notes.

What changes the price of a sewer inspection

An inspection is a short, defined job. These factors decide how long it takes and what you receive.

AccessA cleanout makes the run simple, while a pulled toilet takes more time.
Length of the lineA longer run takes more cable and more review time.
Cleaning firstA heavily coated line may need cleaning before the camera can see.
RecordingA saved video or a written note helps with a home purchase or an insurance claim.
TimingA scheduled visit and a same-day visit may be priced differently.

Insurance rarely pays for a camera inspection on its own, but a saved video can support a claim if a covered event damaged the line. For a home purchase, the cost often fits inside the negotiation.

Inspection for a Dixon, Vacaville or Davis home purchase

Buyers in Dixon, Vacaville and Davis can schedule a camera inspection during the inspection period. Sellers sometimes order one first, so the buyer sees a clean video and the sale stays on schedule.

If the line passes, you have a baseline and a video. If it does not, you can negotiate a repair credit, and a plumber can quote the work from the same video. Either way you buy the house knowing the part nobody can see.
