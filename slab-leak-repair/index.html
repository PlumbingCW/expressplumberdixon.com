When a slab leak is an emergency

Emergency slab leak repair in Dixon, CA applies when water is coming up through the floor, a hot spot has appeared in a room or the meter runs while everything is off. A slab leak is a pressurized pipe leaking under or inside the concrete foundation, so it keeps running until it is shut off.

Our line is answered 24/7. While you wait, shut off the main water valve to stop the flow, and turn off the water heater if hot water is involved. Move rugs and furniture off wet floors, and keep power off in any room where water reached outlets.

Slab leak signs, paraphrased from what callers describe

Callers describe slab leaks in a handful of ways. Any two of these together are a good reason to call.

A warm or hot patch on the floor

A hot water line under the slab is leaking.

Water coming up near baseboards or floor seams

Pressure is pushing water along the easiest path to the surface.

The sound of running water in a quiet house

Water is moving where no fixture is open.

A meter that keeps turning with everything off

A line is leaking somewhere past the meter.

Damp carpet or a musty smell

Moisture is wicking up from below.

Not every one of these means a slab leak. A leak detection visit confirms where the water is really coming from.

Dixon's rules for pipe under a slab

Source type: City of Dixon Building Division water line handout.

The City of Dixon's water line repair and replacement handout sets the ground rules for supply pipe near and under a building. Below the building, the pipe is expected to be Type L copper, and copper under a slab is to have no joints.

The handout also says PVC and polyethylene are not allowed for water supply, calls for tracer wire on buried plastic, and sets 12 inches of cover. A pressure test at working pressure for 15 minutes is part of the check. If city connections or street work are involved, an encroachment permit applies.

The practical point for a slab leak is that a repair or reroute has to meet those rules. That affects the materials used, the number of joints in the finished work and whether the job needs a permit and inspection.

A slab repair follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The City of Dixon's water line handout cites section 604.1 and calls for a 15 minute pressure test at working pressure after the work.

Repair paths for a slab leak

There are three common ways to fix a slab leak. The right one depends on the pipe, the location of the leak and how many leaks there have been.

What to do before we arrive at a slab leak

A leak under the floor is quiet, but the water has to go somewhere. These steps protect the house and the people in it.

  • Close the main water valve to stop the flow.
  • Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances standing in water.
  • Switch off the breaker for any room with standing water if you can reach it dry.
  • Keep children and pets off wet or warm floors.
  • Photograph damage before you move things.
  • Do not run taps while you wait.

How a slab leak is located and fixed

A good slab leak repair starts with proof. Cutting concrete without locating the leak is a mistake that costs money twice.

  1. Confirm the leak

    A meter test and a pressure test show that the line is losing water.

  2. Pinpoint the spot

    Electronic listening and thermal imaging narrow the location to a small area.

  3. Choose the repair path

    You hear the options and the price before anything is cut.

  4. Repair and test

    The pipe is repaired or rerouted, pressure tested, and the floor is restored or handed off to a flooring contractor.
