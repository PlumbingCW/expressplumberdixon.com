Sewer backup: what to do first

Emergency sewer line cleaning in Dixon, CA starts with limiting the damage. Our 24/7 line is open all night, and these steps are worth doing before a plumber arrives.

  1. Stop all water use

    Toilets, sinks, showers, the dishwasher and the washing machine all feed the same blocked line.

  2. Keep people and pets out

    Backed-up wastewater is unsanitary, so keep children and animals away from the wet area.

  3. Shut off power to wet areas

    If water reaches outlets or appliances, turn off the breaker for that room before you step in.

  4. Find the cleanout

    Tell the dispatcher where the cleanout is, because that is the fastest way into the main line.

What a main sewer line does and why it clogs

Every drain in the house joins a single pipe that runs out of the building and meets the sewer lateral on its way to the city main. A blockage anywhere on that shared pipe stops every fixture upstream of it, which is why a main line problem shows up in several places at once.

The usual causes are roots entering at a joint, grease and wipes that collect at a low spot, and a pipe that has sagged or shifted. Cleaning clears the blockage, and a camera shows whether the pipe underneath needs more than cleaning.

Cleanouts and Dixon's sewer lateral rules

Source type: City of Dixon Building Division sewer lateral and permit handouts.

The City of Dixon's sewer lateral handout lists where cleanouts are expected on a service line. They go at the junction of the house drain and the sewer, at the property line and at the end of the line. They also go wherever the pipe changes direction by more than 135 degrees, and at least every 100 feet along the run.

In practice, that means most Dixon lots have a cleanout you can reach, often a capped pipe near the house or at the property line. Finding it before the plumber arrives saves time, and it gives the plumber a straight path into the main line without pulling a toilet.

For permit questions on larger sewer work, the City of Dixon Building Division is the office to ask. The City of Dixon's permit handouts list clearing a stoppage as exempt from a permit, while replacing pipe is not.

The rules behind the cleanout come from the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The City of Dixon's handout cites section 719.1 for where they go and section 710.1 for a backwater valve.

Yard drains and storm drains

Not every drain on a Dixon property connects to the sewer. Yard drains and storm drains collect rainwater and carry it away from the house, and they clog with leaves, silt and mulch rather than grease or paper.

A yard drain that holds standing water after rain can be cleared with a snake or a jetting pass. Tell the dispatcher whether the drain is a sewer cleanout or a yard or storm drain, because they are separate systems and a yard drain should never be treated like a sewer cleanout.
