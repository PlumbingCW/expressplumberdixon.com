Water heater installation in Dixon, planned the right way

Water heater installation in Dixon, CA covers new construction, a heater moved to a new spot, a change of fuel and the upgrade from an old tank to a better unit. These jobs share one advantage over an emergency swap: you have time to choose.

The decision that matters most is where the heater will live. Location sets the vent route, the gas or electrical work, the clearances and whether the City of Dixon's instant permit applies.

Code items on a Dixon install

Source type: City of Dixon Building Division water heater handout.

The City of Dixon's water heater handout cites California Plumbing Code sections that apply to a finished install. Earthquake strapping is called for in section 507.2, with two straps and 4 inches of clearance to the controls. The relief valve discharge goes through hard pipe to the outside, between 6 and 24 inches above grade, under section 504.6, and it may not discharge into a drain pan.

A secondary pan with a three-quarter inch drain is called for where a leak could cause damage, under section 507.5, and attic pans must be at least 1 and a quarter inches deep. Gas heaters need a listed flexible connector no longer than 3 feet, an accessible shutoff and a sediment trap, and a working space of 30 inches. A garage heater's burner has to sit 18 inches above the floor.

Instant permits are for direct replacements only. New gas piping, new electrical work or a change of type means a standard permit, and the city requires the contractor's state license and Dixon business license to be listed.

Heat pump and tankless installs: space, venting and power

A heat pump water heater pulls heat from the surrounding air, so the U.S. Department of Energy says it needs a space between 40 and 90 degrees with about 1,000 cubic feet of air. A tight closet does not qualify, while a garage or utility room often does.

A tankless unit needs enough gas supply and a vent sized for its burner. Many homes with an older tank need a larger gas line and a new vent for tankless, which is why the install costs more than the unit alone. See the tankless water heater page for the detail.

Moving from gas to an electric unit usually means a dedicated circuit, and the city treats any new electrical work as outside the instant permit.
