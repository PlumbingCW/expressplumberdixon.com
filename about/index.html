What we do

Dixon Express Plumbing answers a plumbing line that is open every hour of every day. Callers reach us when a pipe bursts, a drain backs up, a water heater fails or a leak shows on the bill. We ask what is happening, tell you what to do while you wait and arrange a licensed plumber.

Our office is at 2600 Plaza Ct in Dixon, California. From there we cover Dixon, Vacaville and Davis, plus the stretches of I-80 and State Route 113 between them.

How we work

We tell you the price before work starts, and you approve it first. We offer same-day appointments when available, and the dispatcher confirms timing on the call. We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic and other emergencies change it.

Our service pages explain each problem in plain language, including the signs, the steps you can take and the questions to ask any plumber. If a job is better handled by a specialist, we say so.

The area we serve

Dixon sits in Solano County along I-80, with a population of about 21,000. Incorporated in 1878, it grew up around the railroad, and a fire in 1883 destroyed much of the downtown. Rebuilding brought brick buildings that still shape the center of town.

Today the city draws its water from deep groundwater wells and has parks, neighborhoods and farmland on its edges. We use that context when we talk through a problem. Older buildings may have older pipes, and newer subdivisions may have problems tied to water quality or pressure.

Neighbors we cover

Vacaville is about eight miles southwest on I-80. Davis sits where I-80 and State Route 113 meet, northeast of Dixon. Our Vacaville page and Davis page show local water facts and the ZIP codes we cover.

If you live just outside those areas, call anyway. The dispatcher will tell you honestly whether we can help or whether another plumber is a better fit.

What we will not do

We do not publish prices on this website, because they depend on your home. We do not promise arrival times. We do not claim awards, reviews or years in business that we cannot show you.

We do promise to answer the phone, explain the work in plain words and tell you the price before it starts. If we get something wrong, tell us and we will work to fix it.

Local knowledge we use

We read the public reports that affect plumbing here. The City of Dixon's 2024 water quality report lists hardness averaging 163 parts per million and pressure between 52 and 62 pounds per square inch. The City of Dixon Building Division publishes permit handouts for water heaters, water lines and sewer laterals.

That information shapes how we explain problems. Hard water explains scale in heaters and faucets. Permit rules explain why some jobs are exempt and others are not. See our plumbing services page for the full list.

How to check any plumber

In California, the Contractors State License Board licenses plumbing contractors. You can look up a license at cslb.ca.gov or call 800-321-2752. A license is required for work totaling $1,000 or more in labor and materials.

Ask for a written contract, limit any down payment to 10 percent of the price or $1,000, whichever is less, and remember you have three days to cancel, or five if you are 65 or older. These rules apply to any contractor, including us.

Questions people ask first

Callers often ask whether we are open at night. We are, every night. They ask whether we publish prices. We do not, because a number on a web page ignores your home. They ask whether we cover their town. Call and the dispatcher will confirm it.

They also ask how to know a plumber is licensed. Our guidance above applies to anyone you hire. Check the license at cslb.ca.gov before work starts. That one step prevents most of the problems people run into with contractors, and it takes only a minute.
